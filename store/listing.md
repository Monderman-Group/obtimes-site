## Core positioning

On Borrowed Times is an independent journal reader built for sustained reading rather than a stream of alerts. It lets readers search the journal, save essays, resume where they stopped, listen to available audio editions, and keep selected text and audio available offline. Reading activity and preferences remain on the device. The app requires no account and contains no advertising or behavioral reader tracking.

### Review notes

On Borrowed Times is a free, accountless journal reader. No login, demo credentials, subscription, purchase, or external hardware is required.

The app is more than a website wrapper. Its app-specific reader experience includes:

1. A searchable and filterable essay catalogue.
2. A device-local library of saved essays and in-progress reading.
3. Download controls for offline essay text, illustrations, and optional audio.
4. An audio player with saved position, speed control, seeking, and system media controls where supported.
5. Device-local reading settings and a control that clears saved activity and downloads.
6. Native sharing through the operating system’s share sheet.

Suggested review path:

1. Open **Explore** and search for a word or filter by topic.
2. Open an essay and choose **Save for later**.
3. Choose the download control and save the essay for offline reading. If an audio edition is available, include it in the download.
4. Open **Library** to confirm the saved and offline editions.
5. Start an audio edition, seek or change speed, leave the essay, and return to confirm that listening position is retained.
6. Open reading settings to change text size or line spacing, then use **Clear saved data and downloads** to remove device-local activity.

The app retrieves the current catalogue and publication assets from `https://obtimes.com`. A bundled edition provides the app shell and current catalogue fallback. The publisher owns and controls the displayed editorial content. External links open in the system browser. The app does not expose arbitrary web navigation.
